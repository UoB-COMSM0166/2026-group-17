## Reflection on Requirements
Our team’s approach to requirements engineering centered on transforming a high-level vision into actionable small development tasks. By defining Epics, we established the structural pillars of our application, ensuring that the turn-based artillery combat style game remained manageable and aligned with our creative ideas.

The most significant learning occurred during the creation of User Stories. Moving beyond a generic "user," we utilized the "As a..., I want..., so that..." template to empathize with diverse stakeholders. Through team brainstorming and peer feedback from other groups, we identified critical needs for players with color blindness and new players requiring tutorials which we never considered before this workshop. We even considered the Original Pocket Tanks Team as a persona, framing our innovations as modular updates that preserve the game’s core identity while enhancing its commercial potential.

To bridge the gap between these narratives and technical implementation, we also established some Acceptance Criteria (AC). These criteria acted as our "Definition of Done," providing a common language for our team members including developers and designers. For instance, a story for Turn-Based Selection Logic was translated into specific ACs: the system must accurately identify the current player’s turn and lock selected weapons to prevent duplicate picks.

Through this workshop, we successfully clarified the stakeholders of our game and created  important Epics and User Stories (including AC). We have already integrated these into our Kanban board. This structured workflow ensures that every component of our technical implementation is directly tied to a validated user need, keeping our development focused and purposeful.
